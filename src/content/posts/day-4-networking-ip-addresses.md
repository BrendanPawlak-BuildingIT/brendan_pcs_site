This is day four of studying networking every single day. And I have 52 days left to pass my
Network+ or I can lose my job.

Today I learned about IP addresses. Your IP address is the reason data knows where it has to go.

Your IP address is a lot like your mailing address, just for your computer. This first part is like
the name of your street, and this last part is your house number. So it would be like 67 Main
Street.

But that data isn't sent all at once. It actually breaks it into smaller chunks called packets. Each
packet has a header that includes return and destination IP, along with something called time to
live — and that means it has a certain amount of hops before it gets destroyed.

And it sends a lot of packets. And somehow everything arrives at the right house and gets put back
together.
